**Client request:** students ask an AI assistant about their units (for example "when is A3 due?"), and lecturers see which students may need support, based on an ML model. Demonstrate it with **one sample class of five students**.

## Feature A: student course chatbot
- A student signs in, opens MAI600 and asks the chatbot about the unit.
- Answers must be based on the **uploaded unit documents**, not general knowledge, and must **name their source**.
- The brief suggests Chatbase, Groq API + Claude, or local Ollama + Open WebUI. **Any tool is fine as long as the end requirements are met** (team decision, 18 Nov).
- Upload the sample MAI600 knowledge document from the pack, or lecturer-approved unit information.
- **Must answer:** "What is MAI600 about?", "What is required for A3?" and "When is A3 due?"
- **Conflicting documents:** if two documents disagree about a date, the bot must say so and direct the student to the current official Canvas page.
- **Missing answers:** a helpful answer when the documents contain it, and a safe "I cannot confirm" when they don't.
- **Never upload personal student records to a public bot.**

## Feature B: lecturer at-risk prediction
- When the lecturer opens the class, each student row shows an indicator from the trained model, such as **"Review recommended"** or **"Not flagged"**.
- Train separately on the larger fictional dataset (the 400-record `students_demo.csv`).
- **Compare at least two models: a Decision Tree (baseline) and a small MLP neural network** (the deep-learning component).
- Use a proper train/test split. Report **test accuracy and recall for the at-risk class**, with a confusion matrix if possible.
- **Save and load the chosen model.** Score the five demo students through an API or a backend prediction function.
- The lecturer should identify about 1–2 students needing review, **only if the model actually flags them. Never change or fabricate results.**
- Present risk as a **learning-support flag, not proof** that a student will fail. **Never automatically penalise or notify students.**
- Where possible, the lecturer can review the reasons and features behind a flag.

## System boundaries
- **The ML model runs outside the UI** and returns a real prediction. Keep the model file, training code and test results.
- **The chatbot uses only approved course documents** and never invents dates.
- **AI coding help:** Claude may help write code, but must not invent predictions or assessment information.
- **The team lead** owns integration and the end-to-end login tests; each member demonstrates their own component.

## Live demonstration (required, in the real app, not slides)
- **Demo 1, student:**
  1. Log in as a fictional student.
  2. Open MAI600 and show their quiz marks and assignments.
  3. Ask "When is A3 due?" and show the answer and its source; if sources conflict, the bot says so.
  4. Ask one question that is in the notes and one that isn't.
- **Demo 2, lecturer:**
  1. Log in as `mmotahar@asahe.edu.au`.
  2. Open MAI600 and show all five students with their activity data.
  3. Point out the risk indicators.
  4. Open one student and explain which inputs went to the model.
  5. Show the model file or the API response, to prove nothing is hard-coded.
  6. Explain a false negative or another limitation, and how a lecturer should review the result.

## What to submit
- **Working prototype:** student and lecturer logins, with the five demo records and their quiz and attendance data.
- **ML evidence:** a dataset description, training code or notebook, the Decision Tree baseline, the small MLP, test metrics, the saved model and an inference example.
- **Chatbot evidence:** the tool or model used, the unit-note source, 3 test questions, 1 uncertain answer, and screenshots or a short recording.
- **Presentation:** 5–7 minutes covering the problem, data, approach, results, both live demos and limitations. **Every member speaks.**
- **Prototype handover:** a 1–2 page summary of the architecture, working features and contributions.

## Definition of done
- A student signs in and asks about MAI600; the answer is grounded, or the bot honestly says it cannot confirm.
- The lecturer signs in and sees all five records, with risk indicators from the trained model, not UI rules.
- The team can show the training process, the test results, and the data flow from the LMS to the model.
- Fictional data only, role-based access protected, and the limitations of risk predictions explained.
